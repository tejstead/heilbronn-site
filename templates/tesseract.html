The n = 16 tesseract

The optimal placement of 16 points in the unit square (minimum triangle area 7/341, due to Beyleveld 2006) is exactly an affine image of the 16 vertices of a 4-dimensional hypercube. The animation below rotates the tesseract, freezes a 2D projection, then stretches it into the optimal configuration and reveals the 64 minimal-area triangles grouped by their parallelogram structure.

Why a hypercube?

The 16 Heilbronn points have rational coordinates with denominators 31 (x-axis) and 33 (y-axis), 180-degree rotational symmetry, and exactly 64 triangles tied at the minimum area. A least-squares fit to the {0,1}4 vertex set gives machine-epsilon residual: the global optimum lives in the 10-parameter family of affine images of the tesseract, and no relaxation to unconstrained 16-point configurations improves on it.

This structure does not extend to other n = 2d cases: neither the d = 3 (n = 8) nor d = 5 (n = 32) Heilbronn optima are hypercube projections.

Structure of the minimal triangles

The 64 minimal triangles organize into parallelogram groups corresponding to faces of the projected tesseract. Eight of the 24 tesseract 2-faces each contain exactly 4 minimal triangles (shown in blue). Six additional "diagonal" parallelograms, formed by pairs of minimal triangles sharing an edge but not lying on a tesseract face, account for 24 more (shown in orange). Together these cover 56 of the 64 minimal triangles.
